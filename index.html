The flat

Top floor, 3 bedrooms, a terrace, two balconies

Lisbon

Visualisations of the flat with sample furniture. Tap a picture to enlarge it.

Photos of the empty flat as it is today come after you submit your first design. I email them to you, so you can improve your design with them in a second entry.

The flat fills the top floor of a building refurbished in 2023. The living room opens through a 3.5 m glass wall onto a terrace that gets the afternoon sun. Its ceiling slopes from 2.2 m at the glass to 4.2 m under the ridge.

The three bedrooms sit at the back. Two have dormer doors onto small balconies with morning sun. The third looks onto a light well.

The kitchen, both bathrooms and the built-in cupboards stay as they are. Everything else is yours to design.

The brief

Things it must have

  • Main bedroomA bed for a 180 × 200 cm mattress.
  • Home officeA sit-stand desk 120 to 160 cm wide and 70 to 80 cm deep. I work from home and spend long days on calls. I want to have multiple monitors, and I record a podcast.
  • Second bedroomA bed for a 140 to 180 cm mattress, or a sofa-bed. Your choice.
  • My heightCater to my height. I'm 193 cm.

Things I already own

Place these too.

  • DJ controller, Pioneer FLX4, connecting to the laptop via USB-C. 48 × 27 × 6 cm.
  • Office:
    • Laptop, MacBook Pro 14″.
    • 7″ desk ring light by Neewer.
    • Desk arm by Neewer, model TL253A+DS001.
    • Yeti USB microphone.
  • Two kiteboards, 145 × 43 × 5 cm.
  • Two kite backpacks, each about 65 × 40 × 35 cm.
  • A beach bag with kite gear.

What I like

  • Calm, light and Nordic. Few things, chosen well.
  • A TV that stands in a bookshelf rather than on the wall, since the sun is strong. A lot of light comes in from the windows, which affects where the TV can go.
  • A place to DJ standing up. Ideally with good speakers nearby.
  • I live alone. I like having friends visit and stay in a guest room.
  • Read my Kindle on the couch.
  • Eat food. Drink coffee. Sip wine. Maybe a wine fridge somewhere?
  • I like cooking too, but a private chef does my meal prep.
  • Look at the sky.
  • Talk on the phone while walking around the flat.
  • Do something fun and quirky with the storage in each bedroom. Maybe a soundproof room for sensory deprivation?
  • Longevity bro stuff. I am thinking about a cold plunge on the terrace, and maybe compression boots.
  • Yoga and calisthenics. The terrace could hold yoga mats and a dip bar.
  • Move. Stretch.
  • Working somewhere other than the office would be nice, for example from the couch.

Sound and cables

Sound quality matters. The speaker budget is higher than for the rest, for example Sonos speakers around the flat. Speakers can sit inside furniture. The planner has an option for that. I will spend most of my time in the living room and office, so that is where speakers matter most. A basic one in the bedroom and guest room could suffice.

I'm not sure how to get speakers outside, and maybe it's best not to disturb the neighbours too much. Outdoor parties on the terrace would be great though, especially if I can set up a DJ desk outside once in a while.

I want to play music three ways:

  • From my phone, over AirPlay or an app like Sonos.
  • Straight from my computer.
  • Straight from my DJ deck, a Pioneer FLX4. When I DJ, I plug a USB-C cable into the deck and put the Mac behind it. Or I buy a small standalone controller that plays from a USB stick.

I am fine if these are separate systems. But if they all work together, that would be epic.

Bonus points: describe the cables. Show where they run, what plugs into what, and how the deck, computer, TV and speakers connect.

Wet and sandy kite gear

I kitesurf. After a session the gear comes home wet and sandy: two boards, two kite backpacks and a beach bag. It needs a place to drip and dry, close to the front door or the terrace, so the sand stays out of the living room.

Budget

I can rent all the furniture for €600 a month extra, so that is your benchmark. The contract is two years, but I think I will stay for five. The benefit of buying is that I can take it to the next flat, or sell it. I have no liquidity constraints. All constraints are about being money-wise: I don't want to overspend on expensive things if I move after two years and they are hard to sell. I am willing to spend a lot on things I know I will keep for a long time, like the 180 × 200 cm mattress, the office setup, and things I can resell at a low loss.

My rough guess is that this costs at least €7,000 and at most €16,000, but I'm very unsure.

The cold plunge is not included. My €600 a month rental benchmark over two years is €14,400, near the top of this range. A realistic middle is about €10,000 to €12,000. There is no hard cap: spend where it lasts, save where it doesn't.

Willingness to spend is set per item:

  • Sit-stand desk, office speakers and monitors: high.
  • Furniture: medium, IKEA mid-range.
  • A versatile piece I could take to a future flat: medium. Otherwise lower.
  • Plates, knives, glasses and so on: low.

Stores

  • I would buy most things from IKEA or second-hand, but you do you.
  • New furniture could come from IKEA Portugal. I would pick from the mid-range and top-range lines, not the cheapest ones. That gives you a sense of my budget. I am on a two-year lease, so I don't want to overspend on furniture. IKEA pieces are easy to resell, which keeps the flat easy to change later.
  • Second-hand pieces can come from anywhere: Facebook Marketplace, OLX, Vinted, a flea market. I encourage them.
  • Electronics such as a TV or speakers can come from any shop.
  • Bonus points if I can order from amazon.es, as that is my main store.
  • For each second-hand piece, give the listing link, the seller's contact and an IKEA piece to use instead if it sells first.
  • No painting. Prefer freestanding pieces, since anything fixed to a wall needs the landlord's approval.
How to enter

Two ways to send a design

Use the planner

Drag furniture onto the plan

The planner has the flat's dimensions, an IKEA furniture library, a 3D view and a running total. It checks the brief for you.

Press Save and you get a picture of your design and a layout file. Upload both. Open either one later to keep working.

Use your own tools

Send one PDF

For designers with their own software. Send one PDF under 10 MB with a plan to scale and a list of every piece with its size, price and link.

Ask for the plan and dimensions through the question form and I send them to you.

Submit a design

One form per design. Send several designs if you can, each through its own form. You upload your files there, up to 10 MB each.

The prize

Live in the flat while you furnish it

Move in any day from 1 November and move out any day up to 26 November. Stay a weekend or most of the month. It is free.

While you live there, you buy and set up the furniture from your design. The flat is empty on 1 November, so a bed is the first thing to set up if you want to sleep there.

You have the flat to yourself until 5 November. From 5 November I sleep there too, in the main bedroom. I am away from 18 to 26 November, so you have it to yourself again.

I pay for everything in your design. I place and pay for the large orders myself and pay second-hand sellers upfront. For small things, buy them, keep the receipt, and I pay you back. Ask me first for anything over €50 that is not in your design. The receipts let me return anything that does not stay.

If you can't come to Lisbon, you can still win. I then set up the furniture myself from your design.

Your travel to Lisbon is your own cost.

For professionals

Paid instead of the stay

If you design for a living and would rather be paid than stay in the flat, I am open to that. Enter the competition like everyone else, and tell me in your entry what you would want to be paid.

If I choose your design, I pay for the hours you spent on it, at an amount you are happy with. I may want to buy more of your time later, so I have every reason to treat you fairly.

Questions
How do I ask a question?
Use the question form at any time during the competition.
Who judges?
I do. I look at how well the flat would work day to day, how closely it follows the brief and how sensibly it spends the money.
Can professionals enter?
Yes. Students, hobbyists and interior designers are all welcome. See the section for professionals above.
Who owns the design?
You keep the credit. By entering, you let me use your design to furnish this flat and show it with your name.
Do I need to live in Lisbon?
No. You only need to come here if you want to live in the flat and set it up.
